The problem

Right now there is only one way in

To get an appointment at Deepan Hospital today, a patient has to telephone, during working hours, and speak to somebody at the counter. There is no other way.

8am – 8pm

The counter is not open at night or on a Sunday afternoon.

One caller at a time

A second caller hears an engaged tone and rings somebody else.

One language

Whoever picks up decides which language the call happens in.

Nothing in writing

The patient has to remember the time, the doctor and the floor.

Somebody decides at eleven at night that they need a doctor. They go to whichever hospital they can actually reach — and it is not the one whose phone is switched off.
03

Who it is for

Three groups of people use it

Patients

Anyone in Trichy with a phone

They book their own appointment, at any hour, in English, Tamil or Hindi. No account, and no app to install.

Reception

The people at the counter

Bookings appear on their screen by themselves, with a chime. They also book for callers and send each patient the details in writing.

Doctors

The 18 consultants

They keep their consulting hours, fee and room correct. Their clinical work stays in Klinique — this does not touch it.

04

What it does — for a patient

A patient books in about a minute

  1. Opens the website on a phoneIt appears in their own language, without being asked.
  2. Picks a department or a doctorThe fee and the consulting hours are shown before they choose.
  3. Picks a free timeOnly times that doctor actually sits are offered.
  4. Gets a referenceReception sends it to them on WhatsApp, in their language.
A patient never has to sign up, remember a password or download anything. Every extra step is somebody who gives up halfway.

How it works

Three pieces, and a phone

The phone

A patient in Trichy

Any browser. Nothing to install.

The website

What they see and touch

Pages, the booking form, three languages.

The server

A small computer that never sleeps

Decides who gets which slot. Checks everything.

The database

One file that remembers

Every appointment, doctor, fee and hour.

The website is the shopfront. The server is the person behind the counter who checks the diary. The database is the diary itself.
07

The same three pieces, working

Follow one booking, start to finish

11pm

She opens the site on her phone

Her phone downloads the website — pictures, words and buttons. It holds no records at all.

Step two

She taps Orthopaedics

Her phone cannot answer “which times is Dr Deepan free on Thursday?” by itself, so it asks the server.

Step three

The server looks in the diary

It finds the free twenty-minute slots for that doctor on that day, and sends the list back.

Step four

She picks 10:30

She types her name, age, number and reason, and presses Hold this slot.

Step five

The server checks again

Is that slot still free, is that a real ten-digit number. Only then does it write the booking down and send back a reference — DH-WEB01.

Step six

A chime sounds at reception

The booking is on their screen. Nobody typed it, and it took her under a minute.

The diary itself refuses two bookings for the same doctor at the same minute — so even two people tapping in the same second cannot double-book.
08

In plain words

What each piece actually is

The website

Built with React. It is only pictures and words — it holds no records and cannot be trusted with any, because it runs on the patient's own phone where anybody could change it.

The server

Built with Node and Express. It is the only thing allowed to say yes. Every rule lives here — so even if somebody tampered with the website on their own phone, they would get no further than the counter.

The database

SQLite — a single file on the server's disk. Small, fast, nothing extra to run or pay for. It is copied automatically every six hours.

All three are free and open source. There is no licence to renew and no company that can raise a price later — adding the 26th doctor costs nothing.

09

A word that means two things

Have we built a server? Yes — and not yet

Built — the program

Twenty-nine files, about 6,500 lines, and 109 tests that pass. It handles accounts, the doctor catalogue, appointments, payments, the reception desk and its own backups.

This is finished. It runs today.

Not yet — the machine

A rented computer in Mumbai or Bengaluru for the program to live on, so it is running at three in the morning when somebody books. Roughly ₹6,000 – ₹18,000 a year, plus about ₹1,000 for the domain.

Until this is done, it only runs when somebody starts it.

And there is no second machine for the database. It is one file sitting beside the program, so the whole hospital runs on one small computer — most systems need a separate database server, which doubles the bill and adds another thing that can fail at two in the morning.

10

The data

Where the records live, and who can reach them

Inside India

The server should sit in Mumbai or Bangalore. It is Indian patients' health data under the DPDP Act 2023, and keeping it in the country avoids the whole question.

In the hospital's own account

The domain, the server and the backups registered to Deepan Hospital — not to me. A supplier holding a hospital's patient data is a problem for both sides.

Copied off the machine

A backup is written every six hours. Those copies must also be pulled somewhere else — a backup on the same disk is no protection against that disk failing.

Nothing at risk today

The database holds the doctor roster and no real patients. Everything so far has been built and tested without a single patient record in it.

11

The important one

It does not replace Klinique

This app

  • The public booking page
  • Departments, doctors, fees, free times
  • The reception screen
  • Sends the patient their appointment

Klinique

  • The clinical record
  • Prescriptions and consultation notes
  • Patient history
  • What the doctors already use every day
There is deliberately no second place to write a prescription. Two records that can disagree about one patient is a real danger, and avoiding it was worth more than the convenience.
